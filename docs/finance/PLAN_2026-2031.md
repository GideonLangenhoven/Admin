| AI allowance | 5 000 replies included, R0.15/reply beyond, hard stop at 3x |
| Marketing email allowance | 20 included, R0.10/email beyond |

So: the product is built and deployed, the billing machinery exists and works, and essentially none
of it has been switched on commercially. The first job is not scaling. It is collecting one invoice.

**The single most important structural fact:** Yoco secret keys and Meta WhatsApp tokens are stored
per tenant (`businesses.yoco_secret_key`, `businesses.wa_token`). Operators pay their own card
processing fees and their own Meta message fees, directly. BookingTours never touches customer money
and carries no float, no chargeback liability, and no PSP risk. This is why gross margin comes out
above 92%, and it is unusual for a booking platform. It also means a percentage-of-GMV take rate
cannot be skimmed at the payment layer; it has to be invoiced in arrears (see section 5).

## 2. Cost of operations

### What you actually pay for

Five lines, and only five. Everything else in the stack is free-tier or operator-funded.

| Line | What drives it | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Supabase | Plan + compute size + storage/egress/edge invocations | $25 Pro today, $599 Team when buyers start asking for SSO/SOC2 |
| Vercel | Seats + bandwidth + function CPU across two Next.js apps | $20/seat; image optimisation is off by construction (custom loader) |
| LLM | AI replies x tokens | `deepseek/deepseek-v4-pro` at $0.435/M in, $0.87/M out |
| Email (Resend) | Transactional + marketing volume tier | Contacts live in your own Postgres, so no Audiences plan needed |
| Payment fees | 2.95% on your own subscription collections | Only on money you collect from operators, not on their bookings |

### What you do not pay for, and should keep not paying for

- **Maps.** None exists. Meeting points are plain text plus free `google.com/maps/search` links. Do not let a map library in.
- **Weather.** Windguru and Windy are free embeds; Open-Meteo is keyless. One caveat: Open-Meteo's free tier is licensed non-commercial, so budget about EUR 29/mo for their commercial tier once weather becomes a headline feature.
- **SMS.** Zero SMS anywhere in the codebase. Email plus WhatsApp only.
- **Vector database.** pgvector 0.8.0 is already live with per-tenant exact scan. No Pinecone bill, ever.
- **Customer trip photos.** They sit on Google Drive. Google serves those bytes.

At 592 tenants the entire infrastructure bill is about **R1.04m a year, or R177 per tenant per

hour not spent on the thing that actually costs money.

Two caveats worth naming:

1. The single largest metered line by Y5 is **payment fees on your own billing** (R293k), not compute. Moving operators from card links to debit orders or EFT would recover most of it.
2. The per-tenant infrastructure draw in the model (2 GB Supabase egress, 8 GB Vercel bandwidth, 15k edge invocations per tenant per month) is the softest assumption in the whole document. Re-derive it from real telemetry once 20 real tenants are live.

**Read this carefully, because it is the central finding.** The unit economics are excellent (93%
gross margin, LTV/CAC above 16x, CAC payback under 7 months) and the business still does not reach
profitability inside five years. It needs about **R7.3m of funding** to get to 592 tenants.

The reason is arithmetic, not execution: at R2 400 blended revenue per tenant, this cost structure
needs roughly **48 tenants per employee** to break even once programme spend and overheads are
counted. The plan ends Y5 at 42, which is why EBITDA is still 13% negative with 592 tenants on the
books. South Africa realistically holds a few thousand addressable operators (SATSA lists about
1 150 quality-assured products; the wider SMME base is larger but harder to reach and less able to
pay R2 000/mo). A subscription-only, South Africa-only business tops out somewhere around
**R16m to R20m ARR**. That is a good business. It is not one that funds a 14-person team comfortably.

### Growth and churn assumptions behind the curve

- Gross new tenants per year: 26, 58, 122, 197, 306. Founder-led selling in Y1; the first Account Executive arrives at 140 tenants.
- Monthly logo churn: 1.5% falling to 1.0%. A system that hosts the operator's live booking site is stickier than typical SMB SaaS.
- Seasonality: adds peak Sep to Mar, collapse in December. Between 8% and 14% of tenants use the existing self-service pause in winter, which the pro-rated invoice generator already handles correctly.
- Blended subscription drifts from R2 600 down to R2 250 as a lower tier becomes necessary to reach past roughly 200 operators.

## 5. The change that makes it profitable

A **platform fee per paid booking**, invoiced in arrears alongside the existing subscription line.

Most of the mechanism exists. `paid_booking_events` records `(business_id, booking_id, period_key)`
and is already counted for quota enforcement. The invoice generator already computes two usage lines
in exactly this shape (marketing email overage, AI reply overage). Adding a third needs no change to
payment rails, no payment splitting, and no becoming a payment facilitator.

**One prerequisite, and it is not optional.** That table is a quota meter, not a revenue meter. The
trigger `ck_after_booking_paid_status` stamps a row when a booking reaches PAID and nothing ever
reverses it. Live right now: 86 rows stamped, but only 60 bookings are currently PAID or COMPLETED,
**30 stamped bookings have since been cancelled or refunded**, and 4 paid bookings have no row at
all. Billing R7 per row as it stands would over-charge by roughly 50% on reversed bookings and
silently miss others. Before this becomes a money line, either bill off `bookings` filtered by status
and period, or add a reversal row on refund and cancellation. Deciding that a later-cancelled booking
still counts is defensible, but it has to be a deliberate, documented decision, not an artefact of an
append-only trigger, because operators will check it against their own numbers.

At R7 per paid booking, on the same tenant curve and the same team:

| | Y1 | Y2 | Y3 | Y4 | Y5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Exit ARR | R842 143 | R2.61m | R6.31m | R12.31m | R21.70m |
| **Revenue** | **R533 949** | **R2.00m** | **R5.03m** | **R10.34m** | **R18.84m** |
| Gross margin | 94.3% | 96.3% | 95.9% | 94.2% | 93.9% |
| **EBITDA** | **R-638 416** | **R-506 481** | **R-517 009** | **R5 246** | **R2.73m** |
| EBITDA margin | -119.6% | -25.4% | -10.3% | 0.1% | 14.5% |
| Cumulative cash | R-638 416 | R-1.14m | R-1.66m | R-1.66m | R1.07m |

Peak funding need falls from **R7.3m to R1.86m**, profitability arrives in Y4 instead of never, and
Y5 ARR rises 33%. Nothing else in the plan changed.

For the operator, R7 on a R605 booking is 1.2%. That is well below what FareHarbor, Peek or Rezdy
charge, and those platforms typically pass the fee to the traveller rather than the operator, which
is also available here.

| Lever | Effect | Effort |

### The strategic ceiling

Even with the booking fee, South Africa alone caps out around R22m ARR at 592 tenants. Getting
materially past that means one of: geographic expansion (Namibia, Botswana, Kenya share the operator
profile and the language), moving up-market to multi-location operators at R6 500+, or a channel
play through OTA integrations that are already built (Viator, GetYourGuide). That is a Y4 decision,
but the multi-currency and multi-timezone groundwork is a Y3 engineering item.

### Stage 1: 1 to 25 tenants (now to about Aug 2027). Team: founder, then +1 support at 18.

**The goal is not growth. It is proving one operator will pay you every month, twice.**

Engineering, in priority order:

1. **Recurring billing and dunning.** Invoice generation is manual and SUPER_ADMIN-triggered; no cron schedules it. `platform_invoices.status` and `subscriptions.status` are completely disconnected, so an unpaid invoice triggers nothing. `PAST_DUE` is documented in a migration and referenced by zero lines of code. Your one live tenant is sitting in exactly that state right now. Also close the fail-open in `app/lib/api-auth.ts:62`, where a tenant with no subscription row defaults to ACTIVE.
2. **Shared booking deployment cutover.** The code shipped (`booking/app/lib/tenant-server.ts` resolves tenant per request from the Host header). What remains is DNS: point wildcard `*.booking.bookingtours.co.za` plus custom domains at one deployment, verify against several real subdomains, then drop `NEXT_PUBLIC_BUSINESS_ID`. Until this is done, every new tenant is a deployment, and that is the hard ceiling on onboarding throughput.
3. **Self-serve onboarding.** WhatsApp and Yoco credentials are configured by hand today (11 of 13 and 12 of 13 tenants). At 25 tenants that is annoying; at 100 it is a full-time job. This is what the support hire needs before they arrive.
4. **Decommission the legacy global WhatsApp functions.** Six remain deployed, including an unauthenticated `wa-send`.
5. The cheap cost fixes, each under an afternoon: recompress the nine email header PNGs (about 10 MB down to 1 MB, the largest recurring egress source), add `s-maxage` to `/api/img`, put `fetch-google-reviews` behind a shared secret and move it to weekly before any tenant configures a Place ID (it bills at the most expensive Places SKU), add `images: { unoptimized: true }` to the admin `next.config.ts` as a fence.
6. Fix the `/api/img` SSRF allowlist: it uses bare-suffix `endsWith`, so `evilsupabase.co` passes.

Commercial: get BT-2026-07-0003 paid. Decide on the booking fee and the onboarding fee before tenant
two signs, because retrofitting pricing onto existing tenants is far harder than starting with it.

**What breaks if you skip this stage:** you onboard tenants faster than you can deploy for them, and
you have no way to make a non-paying tenant stop consuming resources.

---

### Stage 2: 25 to 75 tenants (about Y2). Team: +engineer at 40.

1. **VAT.** Trailing 12-month turnover crosses R1m around **March 2028 at roughly 57 tenants**, at which point South African VAT registration is compulsory. There is no VAT logic anywhere in the invoicing path: no VAT number field, no VAT line, no tax-invoice compliance. This has a hard legal date attached to it, so build it with the recurring billing work in Stage 1 rather than discovering it late.
2. **Paused tenant cost shedding.** Pause is built and bills correctly, but no cron checks `subscription_status`, so a paused tenant still consumes Resend sends, embeddings and edge invocations. Worse, their public booking site keeps taking bookings and payments. Gate the storefront and skip paused tenants in `cron-tasks`, `auto-messages`, `marketing-dispatch` and `reminder-scheduler`.
3. **Booking fee metering line** in the invoice generator. Fix the meter first: `paid_booking_events` is append-only and never reverses on refund or cancellation, so today 30 of its 86 rows point at bookings that are no longer paid (see section 5).
4. **Extend `check-security-drift` to diff policy predicates, not just grants.** As it stands it would have passed the four cross-tenant P0/P1 holes found in July silently. This is the process gap, and it matters more than any individual fix.
5. Remove the hardcoded Cape Kayak branding that leaks to every tenant: the booking-confirmation address block in `send-email`, the maps URL in `app/new-booking`, and the Google review deep links in three separate functions.
6. Fix the Broadcasts weather-cancel path, which is a second client-side implementation that updates `slots` with no `business_id` filter and never sets `refund_status: ACTION_REQUIRED`, so paid customers cancelled that way silently get no refund option.

**Hire trigger detail:** the engineer at 40 tenants exists because at that point support load plus
roadmap plus incident response no longer fit in one person alongside selling.

### Stage 3: 75 to 180 tenants (about Y3). Team: +senior engineer at 80, +support at 110, +AE at 140.

This is the stage where the platform stops being a product and starts being infrastructure.

1. **Database performance work**, all already identified: 48 multiple-permissive-policy warnings to consolidate, 68 unindexed foreign keys, the remaining N+1 in `cron-tasks:autoTagContacts` (pagination made it correct, it is still a per-tenant cost multiplier), and the `bookings/page.tsx:303` slot-ID prefetch still capped at 1 000.
2. **Add the missing unique constraint** on the `auto-messages` re-engagement path, which is check-then-insert today and will duplicate WhatsApps when a sweep overlaps a tick.
3. **POPIA formalisation**: create the `popia-exports` bucket that code already references, annual penetration test, cyber liability cover. Budgeted from Y2 in the model.
4. **On-call and observability.** Two engineers is the minimum for a rotation that does not burn one person out.
5. **Support tooling**: the admin help chatbot and RAG over `docs/admin-help` already exist, so keep those articles current as features ship, and re-run the sync. That is what keeps the support-to-tenant ratio at 1:150 instead of 1:60.
6. Multi-currency and multi-timezone groundwork, if geographic expansion is on the table for Y4.

### Stage 4: 180 to 350 tenants (about Y4). Team: +platform engineer at 180, +finance/ops at 230, +eng lead at 280, +frontend at 330.

## 8. Risks, and what I would not assume

- **The revenue ramp is the weakest number here.** One paying tenant is not a sales channel. Everything downstream of "26 tenants in Y1" is arithmetic; the 26 itself is judgement, and it is unproven.
- **R2 000/mo may be above what the average South African operator will pay.** The retired `starter` tier at R1 500 exists for a reason. If real-world resistance shows up, blended ARPA falls faster than modelled and the tenant target has to rise to compensate.
- **Per-tenant infrastructure draw is an estimate.** Re-derive it at 20 real tenants. If it is 3x my estimate, COGS triples and gross margin still exceeds 80%, so this risk is bounded.
- **Churn at 1.0 to 1.5% monthly is optimistic for SMB tourism**, an industry with real business failure rates. Seasonal operators may churn rather than pause. Watch the pause-versus-cancel ratio from the first winter.
- **Concentration risk today is total.** One tenant, past due.
- **The model assumes no funding rounds, no debt and no interest.** Cumulative cash is simply the running sum of EBITDA. Add financing rows before showing this to an investor.
- **Open-Meteo's free tier is licensed non-commercial** and you are a commercial SaaS calling it. Small money, real exposure.

## 9. Re-running this

```
node docs/finance/model.mjs           # regenerate every table above
node docs/finance/model.mjs --check   # self-checks only
```

Assumptions are named constants at the top of the file, tagged `[v]` verified against a vendor
pricing page, `[d]` from the live database, `[e]` estimate. The `[e]` values are the ones to argue
with. Self-checks assert that revenue components sum, EBITDA reconciles, cash equals cumulative
EBITDA, seasonality does not distort annual totals, headcount never goes backwards, and the Resend
tier lookup is correct.
