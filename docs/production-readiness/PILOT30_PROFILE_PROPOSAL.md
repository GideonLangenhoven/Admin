# BT30 real-payment pilot proposal

Status: **PROPOSED — NOT APPROVED FOR PUBLIC USE** (revised 28 September 2026). This is a separate `BT30-REALPAY-PILOT-V1` profile. It does not change or pass `BT500-LAUNCH-V1`; see [the release contract](03_RELEASE_CONTRACT.md) and [current state](STATE.md).

## Owner scope

- Up to 30 distinct staff/operator accounts, with all 30 active at the busiest time for most of an eight-hour workday. The pilot accepts real customer bookings and payments. Ten synthetic businesses with three staff accounts each satisfy the current per-business seat rule for qualification; production enrollment may differ but remains capped at 30 staff.
- No new hosting subscription. Ordinary payment-provider transaction fees are allowed. Other billable resources, messages and infrastructure need separate approval. Existing Supabase remains the database/backend.
- Current live sites remain on their existing aliases until a separate deployment decision. The seven reviewed database migrations are already applied; candidate app and Edge versions are not deployed or qualified.

## Proposed acceptance work

1. Freeze exact pilot source, enabled features, provider modes, public traffic bounds, 30-user action traces and total monthly usage estimate. Keep tenant/role and payment correctness standards for every enabled journey. Any excluded feature must be inaccessible in the pilot and stated to its users.
2. Verify genuine sandbox payment, refund and notification journeys, signed/duplicate/reordered callbacks, timeout and unknown-outcome reconciliation, booking capacity/last-seat contention, tenant isolation, Auth recovery and private-file access. Approve and bound any later live payment or message checks separately.
3. Demonstrate 30 unique authenticated staff and 30 active sessions, ramping 5 → 15 → 30. Proposed target: 3 meaningful actions/second for 60 minutes at 70% reads, 20% legitimate writes and 10% other workflows; a five-minute 6-action/second spike; recovery within ten minutes. Include public booking, payment callbacks, background jobs and real Realtime topology. Freeze endpoint fan-out and thresholds before the run. Retain the contract's applicable webhook acknowledgement, payment application, Realtime update, transactional queue and burst-recovery timers, plus at least 30% sustained bottleneck and quota headroom; any BT30 adjustment needs explicit acceptance before qualification.
4. Run a continuous 24-hour soak retaining 30 authenticated connection footprints and a documented realistic cycle that includes **eight hours at the qualified target activity**. Verify financial and tenant invariants during and after load. Do not substitute cached assets or idle sockets for meaningful work.
5. Prove a received failure/financial/queue/quota alert and an isolated recovery of database, Auth, private object bytes, essential protected configuration, app/VM configuration and Edge dependencies. Rehearse a reviewed app/Edge rollback compatible with the applied migrations. The proposed RPO ≤ 15 minutes and RTO ≤ 60 minutes remain gates unless the owner explicitly accepts different objectives in a revised profile before qualification.
6. After the non-production gates, independent review and exact limited-canary approval, verify deployed source/configuration and run a 24-hour controlled canary, expanding staff 5 → 15 → 30 only on observed health. Preserve pending payments, bookings and messages if rollout stops. Full public expansion follows a passing canary and a separate release decision.

The existing [release-contract thresholds](03_RELEASE_CONTRACT.md) for relevant latency, valid-request failures and zero financial/security invariant violations remain the starting proposal. Any BT30 change needs a dated rationale and explicit acceptance before qualification. Source CI, hosted migration success and local restore checks alone do not approve this pilot.

## Zero-subscription hosting decision

The current [Vercel Hobby](https://vercel.com/docs/plans/hobby) team is restricted to noncommercial use; a Pro trial expires. [Netlify Free](https://www.netlify.com/blog/introducing-netlify-free-plan/) permits commercial sites without a card, and the owner's signed-in [Free team had 300/300 credits](evidence/BT30_NETLIFY_FREE_BASELINE_2026-09-28.json) on 28 September. Its [300-credit monthly hard limit](https://docs.netlify.com/manage/accounts-and-billing/billing/billing-for-credit-based-plans/how-credits-work/) pauses sites at exhaustion. Netlify is the current **measurement candidate**, with a proposed 210-credit operating ceiling across all three sites to retain 30% reserve. A previous **illustrative, unmeasured** forecast treated every staff write as a 1-GB/250-ms Netlify function and reached 354.12 credits over 20 workdays; source inspection suggests many actions instead call Supabase directly. It cannot be used as a measured pass or rejection. OCI Always Free is **blocked** because the owner has no credit card for mandatory signup verification. No free host has passed this pilot workload yet. [Private Netlify Free projects](https://docs.netlify.com/manage/security/secure-access-to-sites/project-visibility/) are visible only to the team owner; an owner-only Preview can check the adapter but cannot serve the 30 staff or receive unauthenticated provider callbacks. Public visibility and provider tests require a later scoped approval and passing security gates.

The proposed 3-action/second rate for eight hours over 20 workdays is **1,728,000 staff actions/month** before customer traffic and background work. For Netlify, measure separately the requests/bytes/function duration caused by each action plus customer and background traffic. An illustrative envelope of three production deploys, 2 GB egress, 200,000 hosted requests and 100,000 function calls at 1 GB/250 ms is **194.44 credits**; 500-ms function calls would exceed the proposed 210-credit ceiling. [Supabase Pro includes](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/billing-on-supabase) two million Edge invocations and five million Realtime messages per billing cycle across the organization. Endpoint fan-out, existing usage and remaining allowance are not measured, so 30 accounts alone do not establish ZAR 0 operation; Spend Cap can restrict service when included usage runs out.

The existing project was read-only observed on a Pro organization with an 8-GB disk on 27 September ([sanitized receipt](evidence/BT30_SUPABASE_BASELINE_2026-09-27.json)). The actual compute SKU, monthly usage and billable headroom remain unverified; Pro membership does not identify the running compute size. Confirm Compute & Disk and Billing/Usage in the signed-in dashboard before sizing or provisioning anything else.

The [Oracle testing inquiry](PILOT30_ORACLE_TESTING_INQUIRY.md) is unsent and inactive while OCI signup is blocked. It would need resolution if OCI becomes available later. Netlify's Next.js middleware, Node route handlers, trusted client IP and native image packaging need actual adapter/runtime proof before its candidate can handle public traffic.

[Supabase Pro's included backups](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/backups) are daily, so they do not establish the proposed 15-minute recovery point; a daily backup can lose nearly a day of new bookings. Its [PITR add-on](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/manage-your-usage/point-in-time-recovery) has a separate charge outside Spend Cap and requires at least Small compute. No such purchase is authorized. A measured, encrypted, consistent, offsite recovery process within existing free allowances must prove the **maximum age of completed recoverable points**, not just how often a backup starts. It must cover database, Auth, private object bytes and protected configuration and meet RTO with financial reconciliation. Alternatively, an explicitly revised recovery objective still needs private-file, RTO and reconciliation proof before this pilot can pass recovery.

The immediate implementation path is isolated host-portability work, account/Always Free capacity checking and a permitted measured 30-user rehearsal on a commercially eligible free host. Do not point public domains or send real payments until the non-production gates pass and the owner approves the exact limited-canary action and window.
