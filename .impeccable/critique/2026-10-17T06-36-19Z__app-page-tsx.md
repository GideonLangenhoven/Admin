**What works**

- Departure grouping keeps time, activity, booking count and guest count together, including simultaneous tours.
- Payment and waiver prerequisites are explained in words. Check-ins clearly states that recording a received payment does not charge a card.
- The restrained palette and persistent business identity create a calm, capable working environment.

**Five priorities**

1. **[P1] Put booked guests first.** Visually confirmed on desktop: Simple view places its large introduction, day controls, other availability and payment recovery before booked-session guests. The full dashboard puts the pax/revenue band ahead of manifest and roll call. Move the next departure and its guests immediately below compact day controls; make revenue secondary and collapse recovery to an actionable count. Sources: app/simple/page.tsx:83 and app/page.tsx:639. Suggested commands: `$impeccable layout`, `$impeccable distill`.

2. **[P1] Keep the workspace mounted during refresh.** Source confirms that the 60-second timer and booking updates call `load()`, which sets the loading state and replaces the screen with skeletons. This can interrupt scanning and remove a focused control. Separate initial loading from background refresh, preserve departure selection and focus, and show saving feedback on the affected arrival row. Source: app/page.tsx:482. Suggested command: `$impeccable harden`.

3. **[P1] Explain and support partial arrivals.** The dashboard’s round checkbox acts on the entire booking; its handler records everyone or zero. A partial group has no visible mixed indicator there, and the count editor lives on a different screen. Keep an explicit “All arrived” action, show “1 of 3 arrived” beside it, and provide an adjacent “Edit count” action. Source: app/page.tsx:536. Suggested commands: `$impeccable clarify`, `$impeccable harden`.

4. **[P2] Route payment blockers to payment resolution.** Both assessments confirmed that dashboard balance-related “Open booking” links point to `#waiver`. The explanation identifies one problem, then lands the operator in another section. Reuse Simple view’s blocker-aware destination and use specific labels such as “Record payment” or “Resolve waiver.” Source: app/page.tsx:969; existing pattern: components/simple/DepartureCard.tsx:97. Suggested command: `$impeccable clarify`.

5. **[P2] Simplify departure selection in New Booking.** Source-only finding: a calendar, slot legend, five-day availability matrix and later departure dropdown present several models for one decision. The matrix also has a 760px minimum width and hidden scrollbar. Default to date plus available times; reveal “Compare nearby dates” when needed and preserve the preselected walk-in departure. Source: app/new-booking/page.tsx:1003. Suggested commands: `$impeccable distill`, `$impeccable adapt`.

**Cognitive load and emotional journey**

The main failures are single focus, hierarchy and progressive disclosure. Grouped navigation is manageable; repeated departure-selection controls demand more interpretation. The experience begins calmly, loses momentum while staff search for guests or resolve blockers, then regains confidence through explicit payment/waiver explanations and booking receipts.

**Persona red flags**

- **Experienced operator:** unnecessary scanning before arrivals, whole-group-only controls, and refresh interruptions disrupt desk rhythm.
- **First-time operator:** Today, Roll call and Check-ins describe overlapping work; a checkbox beside a three-person booking does not visibly explain its effect.
- **Distracted mobile operator — source inference:** the horizontally scrolling booking matrix and long expanded guest details are likely friction. Core touch targets are generally generous, but mobile rendering was not verified.

**Smaller improvements**

Give the selected manifest departure a persistent visual state. Replace hover-only customer notes and the emoji marker with an accessible disclosure. Keep an active navigation cue on Check-ins. Rename the Revenue panel to reflect its actual measure—booking value—so staff do not have to reinterpret it as distinct from cash received.
